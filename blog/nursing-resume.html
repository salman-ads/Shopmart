Nursing Resume: Examples, Skills & Writing Guide for 2026

By ShopMart Team • March 6, 2026 • 9 min read

A nursing resume is highly specialized. Unlike most other professions, your resume needs to immediately prove your clinical experience, your state licenses, and your ability to handle high-pressure patient care. Hospital recruiters and automated screening systems have no time to waste—they need to see your qualifications instantly.

In this guide, we will break down the perfect nursing resume structure, the top clinical skills to include, and provide real examples for both Registered Nurses (RNs) and new graduate nurses.

The 5 Essential Sections of a Nursing Resume

Your resume must be perfectly structured for quick scanning. Use this exact order:

  1. Contact Information: Name, phone, email, city/state (no full address), and your LinkedIn profile.
  2. Licenses & Certifications: (Crucial!) Your RN license number, state, expiration date, BLS, ACLS, PALS, etc. Put this at the top.
  3. Professional Summary: A 2-3 sentence pitch highlighting your specialty (e.g., ER, ICU, Pediatrics) and years of experience.
  4. Clinical Experience: Your work history in reverse-chronological order. Focus on patient ratios, specialized procedures, and charting systems (Epic, Cerner).
  5. Education: Your nursing degree (BSN, ADN), university, and graduation date.

Top Hard Skills for Nursing Resumes

Hospitals use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to search for specific clinical keywords. Make sure your resume includes a dedicated "Skills" section with terms like these:

  • Patient Assessment & Triage
  • IV Therapy & Phlebotomy
  • Wound Care & Dressing Changes
  • Medication Administration (PO, IV, IM)
  • Electronic Health Records (EHR)
  • Epic / Cerner / Meditech
  • Vital Signs Monitoring
  • Patient & Family Education
  • Infection Control Protocols
  • Emergency Response / Code Blue

Registered Nurse (RN) Resume Example

Here is how to write impactful bullet points for your clinical experience:

Registered Nurse (RN) - Emergency Department

City General Hospital | Jan 2022 – Present

  • Provide direct nursing care to a 1:4 patient ratio in a 30-bed Level II trauma center.
  • Collaborated with multidisciplinary teams to implement treatment plans, reducing patient wait times by 15%.
  • Administered medications and IV therapies via Epic EHR system with a 100% medication administration accuracy rate.
  • Precepted and mentored 5 new graduate nurses during their 12-week orientation.

New Graduate Nurse Resume Example

If you are a new grad, your clinical rotations act as your "experience." Here is how to format it:

Clinical Rotations

State University School of Nursing | Aug 2023 – May 2025

  • Medical-Surgical Unit (180 hours): Managed care for 5-6 patients per shift, performing assessments, wound care, and medication administration.
  • Pediatrics (90 hours): Monitored pediatric vital signs and assisted with family education and discharge planning.
  • ICU (90 hours): Observed and assisted with ventilator management, arterial line monitoring, and sedation protocols.

3 Common Nursing Resume Mistakes

  • Forgetting your license number: If your license isn't clearly visible at the top of the page, you will be auto-rejected.
  • Using a fancy, multi-column template: Hospital ATS systems (like Taleo or Workday) will scramble your text. Stick to a clean, single-column format.
  • Listing duties instead of outcomes: "Responsible for patient care" is too vague. Write "Managed care for 6 acute patients, maintaining a 95% patient satisfaction score."

Conclusion

Your nursing resume is a clinical document. It needs to be precise, highlight your certifications, and prove your ability to handle patient care. By following this structure, you will pass the hospital's ATS software and impress the nursing recruiter.
