How to Save Your Resume as PDF (ATS-Safe) in 2026

By ShopMart Team • February 18, 2026 • 8 min read

Saving your resume in the wrong file format is one of the most common — and most overlooked — job application mistakes. A resume that looks perfect in Word can arrive corrupted, misaligned, or unreadable on the recruiter's side if you don't choose the right format.

In this guide, we'll explain why PDF is almost always the best format, how to save your resume as an ATS-safe PDF, and what common mistakes to avoid.

Why PDF is the Best Resume Format

Out of all file formats (Word, PDF, Pages, RTF), PDF is the safest choice for 95% of job applications because:

  • Preserves formatting — Fonts, spacing, and layout look the same on every device.
  • Universally accepted — Every ATS and recruiter can open a PDF.
  • Can't be accidentally edited — Your document stays exactly as you designed it.
  • Small file size — Easy to email and upload.

The only exception: when the job posting explicitly says "submit as Word (.docx)." In that case, follow their instructions.

How to Save Your Resume as PDF (Step-by-Step)

If You're Using Microsoft Word:

  1. Open your resume in Word.
  2. Click File → Save As.
  3. Choose a location and type your filename: FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf.
  4. Under "Save as type," select PDF (*.pdf).
  5. Click Options and check "Document structure tags for accessibility" (important for ATS).
  6. Click Save.

If You're Using Google Docs:

  1. Open your resume in Google Docs.
  2. Click File → Download → PDF Document (.pdf).
  3. The PDF will download automatically.
  4. Rename the file: FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf.

  1. Click the Download button.
  2. Your resume is automatically exported as an ATS-safe PDF — no settings required.

How to Make Sure Your PDF is ATS-Safe

Not every PDF is ATS-friendly. A "scanned" or image-based PDF will fail parsing. Here's how to verify yours:

  1. Open your PDF in any PDF reader.
  2. Select all text (Ctrl+A / Cmd+A).
  3. Copy and paste it into a plain text editor (Notepad, TextEdit, etc.).
  4. Check the result:
    • ✅ If the text appears in correct order → your PDF is ATS-safe.
    • ❌ If text is scrambled, missing, or in jumbled order → the ATS will fail on it too.

Why this works: If you can highlight and copy text from the PDF, so can an ATS. If you can't, it means the resume is saved as an image — which ATS cannot read.

Common PDF Mistakes to Avoid

  • Scanning a printed resume — Scanned documents are images, not text. ATS can't read them.
  • Exporting from a design tool (Photoshop, Canva) — Many of these produce image-based PDFs. Stick with Word, Google Docs, or a resume builder.
  • Using "Print to PDF" — It usually works, but can flatten text and break selectability.
  • Password-protected PDFs — Recruiters can't open them, and neither can ATS.
  • Huge file sizes — Keep your PDF under 1 MB for smooth uploads.
  • Weird filenames — Use FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf, not final_resume_v7.pdf.

PDF vs. Word: Which Should You Choose?

Feature PDF Word (.docx)
Preserves formatting ✅ Always ❌ Depends on device
ATS compatibility ✅ Excellent ✅ Excellent
Easy to edit ❌ Hard ✅ Yes
Recommended for Submitting Drafting

Best practice: Keep a Word/Google Docs version for editing, and export a PDF for every submission.

Conclusion

Saving your resume as a PDF is one of the simplest things you can do to improve your job application — but only if you do it right. Always check that your PDF is text-based, not scanned. Use a clean filename. Keep the file size small. And always run the copy-paste test before hitting submit.
