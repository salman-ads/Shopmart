7 Common Resume Mistakes That Get You Rejected in 2026

By ShopMart Team • Updated January 10, 2026 • 7 min read

You might have the perfect skills and years of experience, but if your resume contains common errors, it will never reach a human recruiter. In 2026, Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and automated filters are more ruthless than ever.

We've reviewed thousands of resumes, and we see the exact same mistakes over and over again. Here are the 7 most common resume mistakes that will get you rejected, and how to fix them before you hit submit.

1. Using a Multi-Column or Graphic-Heavy Template

It looks pretty on Instagram, but it will fail in the real world. Many job seekers use templates with two columns, sidebars for contact info, or graphic skill bars.

Why it fails: ATS software reads left-to-right, top-to-bottom. When it hits a two-column layout, it jumbles the text together into unreadable gibberish. If it can't read your resume, it auto-rejects you.

✅ The Fix: Use a clean, single-column layout. Put your contact info at the very top in the main body of the document.

2. Putting Contact Info in the Header/Footer

This is a hidden ATS killer. You might put your phone number and email in the Microsoft Word header section to make it look neat.

Why it fails: Most ATS parsers are programmed to completely ignore the header and footer of a document. If your email is in the header, the recruiter literally cannot see how to contact you.

✅ The Fix: Keep your name, email, and phone number in the main body text at the top of page 1.

3. Missing the Job Description Keywords

Sending the exact same generic resume to 50 different jobs is the fastest way to get zero interviews.

Why it fails: ATS systems score your resume based on how well it matches the job description. If the posting asks for "Project Management" and you wrote "Managed Projects," the system might not give you the points.

✅ The Fix: Tailor your resume for every application. Read the job post and mirror the exact keywords they use in your skills and experience sections.

4. Using Weak or Vague Action Verbs

Phrases like "Responsible for..." or "Helped with..." make you sound like a passive observer, not an active employee.

Why it fails: Recruiters scan for impact. They want to see what you *achieved*, not just what your daily duties were.

✅ The Fix: Start every bullet point with a strong action verb (Led, Built, Increased, Reduced, Streamlined) and include metrics. For example: "Increased sales by 20% by implementing a new CRM system."

5. Submitting a Scanned Image or Flat PDF

If you scan a printed resume, or export a design file as a flat image PDF, you are submitting a picture of your resume.

Why it fails: If you open your PDF and cannot highlight the text with your mouse, the ATS cannot read it either. The system will see a blank page.

✅ The Fix: Always save your resume as a text-based PDF or a .docx file. Run the "Copy-Paste Test" before submitting (copy your text into Notepad—if it looks scrambled, the ATS is blind).

6. Making it Too Long (or Too Short)

A 5-page resume for someone with 3 years of experience is a red flag. So is a 1-page resume for a CEO.

Why it fails: Recruiters spend 6-8 seconds per resume. If it's too long, they will miss your best points. If it's too short, you look underqualified.

✅ The Fix: Keep it to one page if you have under 5 years of experience. Use two pages only if you have a long, highly relevant work history.

7. Typos and Grammatical Errors

Even a single spelling mistake can cost you the job.

Why it fails: 77% of recruiters say they instantly reject resumes with typos. It shows a lack of attention to detail—which is a fatal error for most roles.

✅ The Fix: Read your resume backwards (bottom to top). It forces your brain to slow down and actually read the words, rather than skimming over them. Then, ask a friend to review it.

Conclusion

Your resume is a marketing document. By avoiding these 7 common mistakes, you'll instantly move ahead of 90% of the other applicants. Keep it clean, tailor it to the job, and make sure it's readable by both machines and humans.
