Student Resume Guide: Templates & Examples for 2026

By ShopMart Team • February 2, 2026 • 8 min read

Writing your first student resume can feel impossible when you have little or no work experience. But here's the truth: recruiters hiring students and interns don't expect years of experience. They're looking for potential, initiative, and relevant skills.

This guide will walk you through exactly how to write a student resume that gets noticed — even if you've never had a job before.

What is a Student Resume?

A student resume is a document that highlights your education, skills, projects, and any relevant experiences (part-time jobs, volunteering, internships, or extracurriculars) to help you land your first job or internship.

Unlike professional resumes that lead with work experience, a student resume leads with education and skills — because that's where your strengths are.

The Best Student Resume Format

Use this section order for maximum impact:

  1. Contact Information — Name, phone, email, LinkedIn, portfolio/GitHub
  2. Professional Summary — 2–3 lines describing who you are and what you offer
  3. Education — Degree, university, graduation date, GPA (if 3.0+), relevant coursework
  4. Skills — Technical and soft skills relevant to the job
  5. Projects — Class projects, personal projects, hackathons
  6. Experience — Part-time jobs, internships, volunteering, freelance work
  7. Certifications / Achievements — Awards, online courses, competitions

Pro tip: Use a clean single-column layout. Multi-column designs look nice but confuse ATS parsers.

What to Include (When You Have No Experience)

If you're starting from zero, here's what counts as "experience" on a student resume:

  • Class Projects — Any project where you built, designed, wrote, or analyzed something
  • Volunteer Work — Community service, NGO work, event organizing
  • Part-Time Jobs — Retail, tutoring, food service (shows responsibility)
  • Freelance Work — Design, writing, coding gigs from Upwork or Fiverr
  • Extracurriculars — Student clubs, sports teams, student government
  • Online Courses — Coursera, Udemy, NPTEL certificates

5 Common Student Resume Mistakes

  1. Listing every course — Only include coursework relevant to the job.
  2. Using a photo — In most countries (US, UK, Canada), photos get your resume rejected. Keep it text-only.
  3. Writing generic summaries — "Hardworking student seeking opportunity" tells recruiters nothing. Be specific.
  4. No metrics — Even small numbers add credibility (e.g., "grew club membership by 40%").
  5. Fancy formatting — Multi-column layouts, icons, and graphics break ATS parsing.

How to Tailor Your Student Resume for Each Application

The #1 mistake students make is sending the same resume to every job. Here's how to fix that:

  1. Read the job description carefully — Highlight every skill and requirement.
  2. Mirror the keywords — If they say "Python," don't write "programming."
  3. Reorder your sections — Put the most relevant project or skill first.
  4. Customize your summary — Reference the specific role and company.
  5. Remove irrelevant content — If it doesn't help you land this job, cut it.

Conclusion

Your student resume doesn't need to be perfect — it just needs to clearly show that you're ready to learn, work hard, and contribute. Focus on education, skills, and projects. Add any experience you have, even if it seems small. And always tailor it to the job you're applying for.
