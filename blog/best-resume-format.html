Best Resume Format for 2026 (Templates & Examples)

By ShopMart Team • February 28, 2026 • 9 min read

Choosing the right resume format is one of the most important decisions you'll make in your job search. The wrong format can bury your best achievements, confuse Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), and get your resume rejected before a human even reads it.

In 2026, there are three main resume formats — and one of them is almost always the best choice. Let's break down how to choose the best format for your specific situation.

The 3 Main Resume Formats

Before we decide which is the "best," you need to understand the three options:

1. Reverse-Chronological Format

Lists your work experience from newest to oldest. This is the standard format used by 99% of job seekers.

  • Best for: Anyone with a steady work history and no major gaps.
  • Why: Recruiters love it because it's easy to scan. ATS software parses it perfectly.

2. Functional (Skills-Based) Format

Focuses on your skills and accomplishments rather than a timeline of jobs. Work history is either omitted or placed at the bottom.

  • Best for: Career changers, people with large employment gaps, or candidates with very little experience.
  • Why: It shifts focus away from dates. However, many recruiters and ATS systems dislike it because it feels like you're hiding something.

3. Combination (Hybrid) Format

Combines the best of both worlds. It starts with a "Skills" section at the top, followed by a reverse-chronological work history.

  • Best for: Senior professionals, tech workers, or career changers who want to highlight specific skills without hiding their experience.
  • Why: It shows off your skills but still provides the standard timeline recruiters expect.

Which Format is Best for You in 2026?

Your Situation Best Format
Steady work history Reverse-Chronological
Recent graduate / Student Reverse-Chronological (Education first)
Career changer Combination / Hybrid
Large employment gap Combination / Functional
Senior / Executive Combination / Hybrid

The Verdict: For 90% of job seekers, the Reverse-Chronological Format is the best choice because it is the most ATS-friendly and the most preferred by recruiters.

The Best Resume Layout Rules (2026)

Regardless of which format you choose, you must follow these layout rules to ensure your resume passes ATS screening:

  • Single Column: No sidebars or multi-column designs. ATS reads left-to-right and will jumble multi-column text.
  • Standard Fonts: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, or Inter. No cursive or decorative fonts.
  • Standard Headings: Use "Experience," "Education," and "Skills." Do not use creative titles like "My Journey."
  • No Images or Graphics: Skill bars, photos, and icons break ATS parsers.
  • Margins: Keep margins between 0.5 inches and 1 inch.
  • Length: 1 page for under 5 years of experience. 2 pages max for senior roles.

How to Structure Your Resume (Reverse-Chronological)

Here is the exact order you should use:

  1. Contact Information: Name, phone, professional email, LinkedIn.
  2. Professional Summary: 2-3 lines highlighting your top skills and biggest achievement.
  3. Work Experience: Job title, company, dates, and 3-5 achievement-focused bullet points.
  4. Education: Degree, university, graduation year.
  5. Skills: Hard skills relevant to the job.
  6. Certifications / Projects: Optional, but highly recommended.

Common Formatting Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a Canva/Photoshop template: These often export as image-based PDFs which ATS cannot read.
  • Putting contact info in the header: ATS often skips headers and footers entirely.
  • Adding a photo: In the US, UK, and Canada, photos are a red flag. Keep it text-only.
  • Including age, marital status, or religion: These are outdated and can get your resume discarded.
  • Not saving as PDF: Unless the job post specifically asks for a Word document, always submit a PDF.

Conclusion

The best resume format in 2026 is a clean, single-column, reverse-chronological layout that focuses on achievements. If you're changing careers or have an employment gap, use the combination format instead.

Whichever you choose, prioritize ATS compatibility above all else. A beautifully designed resume that gets rejected by the software is useless.
